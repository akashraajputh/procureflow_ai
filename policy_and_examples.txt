This policy decides what happens to every purchase requisition (PR) line raised in Corvo: whether Procurement
may place a purchase order (PO) directly, or must first run a sourcing event to get quotes. All values are in INR,
excluding GST.
1. Identifying the item
1.1 Every PR line must be matched to an item in the item master. If the requester gave an item code, use it. If
not, match the description to the item master (requesters often use short forms, e.g. "brg 6205 2rs" for Ball
bearing 6205-2RS).
1.2 A line that matches no item master item is a new item. It always goes to sourcing (route
NEW_ITEM_SOURCING ), is never clubbed with other lines, and is valued at quantity × the requester's
estimated unit price.
2. Clubbing requirements (no splitting)
2.1 Splitting a requirement into several small PRs to stay under a limit is not allowed. Procurement must
therefore treat similar PR lines as one requirement and apply every limit in this policy to the requirement's
total value.
2.2 Take PR lines in order of creation time (then PR number, then line number). A line joins an existing
requirement when it is for the same item, for the same plant, was created within 14 calendar days of the
requirement's first line, and its need-by date is within 7 days (either side) of the first line's need-by date.
Otherwise it starts a new requirement. If it could join more than one, it joins the earliest.
2.3 The requirement date is the creation date of its first line. Contract validity and purchase history are
checked as of this date. If any line in the requirement is marked Emergency, the requirement is an
emergency.
3. Valuing a requirement
3.1 Requirement value = total quantity × unit price, where the unit price is the first of: (a) the rate of the
contract that will be used (4.3), or, if every applicable contract is out of ceiling, the lowest applicable
contract rate; (b) the price on the most recent PO for the item in the 365 days before the requirement date;
(c) the item master standard price.
4. Contracts
4.1 A rate contract applies to a requirement if the contract covers the item, the requirement date falls between
the contract's start and end dates (inclusive), and the contract supplier is Active in the supplier master.
4.2 A contract can be used only while it has ceiling left: consumed value + this requirement's value (at the
contract rate) must not exceed the contract value ceiling. Requirements consume ceiling in order of

6. Preferred supplier list
Procurement keeps a preferred supplier list for every item, built automatically from purchase history and contracts
and recalculated as of each requirement date.
6.1 Who is on the list: every Active supplier that has either (a) at least one PO for the item in the 548 days
before the date, or (b) a contract covering the item that started on or before the date (even if it has since
expired).
6.2 Ranking: rank the suppliers on the list so that the best choice for a direct PO comes first. Procurement looks
at: how often we have bought the item from them, how recently, whether they have held a contract for the
item, and their on-time delivery and quality record. Procurement leaves the exact weighting to the system
design, but it must be consistent, explainable to a buyer, and recorded. The top supplier is the 1st preferred
supplier.
6.3 Reference price: the price on that supplier's most recent PO for the item in the 548 days; if it has none, the
rate in its most recent contract for the item.

6.4 Suggesting suppliers for sourcing: take the preferred list in order. If it has fewer suppliers than needed (3
or 5), add other Active suppliers that list the item's category in the supplier master, best delivery and quality
record first. New items get no suggestions: the buyer finds suppliers.
7. Placing purchase orders (consolidation)
7.1 To reduce the number of POs, requirements that end in a PO are combined into one PO with several lines
when they have the same supplier, the same plant and the same route, and were raised within 24 hours
of the first requirement on that PO (measured from the creation time of each requirement's first line).
7.2 Take requirements in order of creation time. A requirement joins the earliest open PO it qualifies for;
otherwise it starts a new PO. Sourcing requests are never combined.
8. Record keeping
8.1 Every decision must record the policy clause applied, and the contract or PO it relied on. A buyer may
override a decision only with a written reason, which is kept with the original decision.
